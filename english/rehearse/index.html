Oral Rehearsal

Model answers are worth reading, but reading them only builds recognition. In the exam you need retrieval, and those are different skills. This module names the moves a strong answer is built from, then makes you recall them until they come without thinking.

Every SBC set is the same three questions

The topic changes; the three jobs do not. Question one asks what you think of the picture. Question two asks for something that happened to you. Question three asks for an opinion you can defend. Each has a different shape, and knowing which one you are in tells you what to say first.

Vocabulary upgrades

The same idea, said better. These are the swaps that lift an answer without sounding rehearsed.

Four ways of remembering, hardest last

Recognising a phrase is easy. Producing it from nothing is what the exam asks for. Work down the list as each one gets comfortable.

Assemble one answer, move by move

Pick a scene and a question. You will write one box per move, with the job of that move in front of you. The phrase tracker shows whether you actually used a signal phrase for each.

Which moves are still shaky

Every drill answer is recorded against the move it tested. The moves you get wrong most often come up first in the drills, so you spend your time where it is worth spending.
