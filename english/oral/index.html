  • Reading Aloud now carries a preamble. SEAB calls it a given situation: one or two lines above the passage telling you the purpose, the audience and the context. It is the reason the component went from 10 marks to 15. Read the same passage as a tour guide and as a news presenter and it should sound obviously different.
  • The stimulus is a real-life photograph. Not a poster, not a drawing. No title, no caption, no text at all, so there is nothing to read off it. Usually ordinary Singaporean life: a hawker centre, a void deck, a school field.
  • The printed sub-prompts are gone. Three free-standing questions, each needing a complete answer you build yourself. Nothing on the page tells you what to include any more.
  • The passage and the conversation are no longer linked. Before 2025 they shared a theme. Now they do not, so do not go hunting for a connection.
  • Marks moved. Paper 4 used to be 30 marks and 15% of the grade. It is now 40 marks and 20%. Any guide still saying 10 + 20 = 30 is describing the old paper.
How marks are given

The bands, plainly

Honest note: SEAB publishes the mark totals and the assessment objectives for Paper 4, but it does not publish band descriptors. What follows is this studio's own rubric, written from the published objectives and from what Singapore teachers consistently reward. It is what the AI marks you against here. Aim at it, but do not quote it as SEAB's.

Reading Aloud

15 marks · three dimensions of 5

Pronunciation and articulation /5

5Every word accurate, including the long ones. Endings and final consonants are audible. No Singlish vowel shortcuts.
3–4Mostly accurate. One or two words slurred or mispronounced, but meaning never gets lost.
1–2Several words wrong or swallowed. The listener has to work to follow.

Fluency, pace and phrasing /5

5Reads in sense groups of two to four words. Full stop, pause for two; comma, pause for one. Steady, unhurried.
3–4Generally smooth, with word-by-word patches or punctuation passed straight over.
1–2Word by word, or fast enough that the punctuation disappears entirely.

Expression and fit to the given situation /5

5The voice clearly matches the stated purpose, audience and tone. Key words stressed, questions rise, the ending falls.
3–4Some expression, but the given situation is only partly reflected.
1–2Flat throughout. The same reading would have suited any situation at all.

Stimulus-Based Conversation

25 marks · each answer scored on three dimensions of 5, then scaled

Ideas and personal response /5

5Answers the question actually asked. Anchors in the photograph, then goes past it with a reason and one specific, bounded example.
3–4On topic with a reason, but the example is a general habit rather than one incident, or the link back to the photo is missing.
1–2A sentence or two, or a well-said point that does not answer the question.

Language and vocabulary /5

5Precise, varied words used naturally. Spoken grammar accurate. Hedges (might, perhaps) where the answer is an inference about people in a photo.
3–4Clear and mostly accurate, but plain; or one large word forced in where it does not sit.
1–2Repetitive, or spoken structures that blur what is meant.

Engagement and delivery /5

5Sounds like a conversation, not a recital. Few fillers. Runs roughly 20 to 40 seconds and finishes on purpose.
3–4Comfortable, but with noticeable "um" and "like", or it trails off without an ending.
1–2Very short, hesitant, or audibly memorised.
